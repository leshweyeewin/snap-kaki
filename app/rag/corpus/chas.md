# CHAS Application Form — field guidance corpus

Grounding corpus for the field-explainer agent. One `##` section per chunk.
Each chunk is retrieved and handed to the model as the ONLY source it may
explain from. If a question is not covered here, the explainer must say it
does not know rather than improvise — that refusal is what keeps the
broad-vision version of this product safe.

Source: "Application for CHAS Subsidies", **Dec 2025 version**, downloaded
from chas.sg. Publicly available, no SingPass needed. Field names, option
wording and section numbers below are taken from that document. Plain-language
restatement, not legal advice.

If chas.sg publishes a newer revision, re-check this file against it before a
demo: the Dec 2025 form already differs from the Jan 2023 one in its dwelling
type options, its rental status wording, and where the declaration sits.

## What CHAS is and how the tier is decided

CHAS is the Community Health Assist Scheme. It is a Singapore government
subsidy card. With it, you pay less at participating GP and dental clinics.
Applying costs nothing and commits you to nothing — you are asking to be
assessed.

All Singapore Citizens are eligible. What differs is the tier, and the tier
is worked out for you from one of two things:

- the **Household Monthly Income Per Person**, for a household that has
  income; or
- the **Annual Value of the home** on the NRIC, for a household with no
  income.

You do not choose your tier on this form and you do not calculate either of
those numbers yourself.

Note: people on the ComCare Long-Term Assistance Scheme (Public Assistance
Scheme) already receive subsidies for conditions CHAS covers and do not need
to apply.

## Why there is no income question on this form

This form never asks you to write down your income. This surprises people and
is a common reason someone thinks they have missed a page. You have not.

Income is not self-declared because the government already holds it. When you
sign the consent in Section 2B, you agree that the Government, IRAS and the
CPF Board may collect, use and disclose your information — including your
income information and your CPF records — to assess which CHAS tier you
qualify for.

Practical consequence: if something asks you to type your salary into a CHAS
form, stop and check you are on the official form.

## Section 1A — Pink NRIC / Birth Certificate Number of the main applicant

Section 1A is about the **main applicant**, and the form states the main
applicant must be a Singapore Citizen. So this field asks for a **Pink NRIC
number**, or a **birth certificate number** for a child too young to have been
issued an NRIC.

Write the number exactly as printed on the card, including the letter at the
start and the letter at the end. Capital letters, no spaces. The boxes on the
form are laid out as one letter, then seven digits, then one letter.

The last letter is a checksum, so a single wrong digit makes the whole number
invalid. This is the field most worth checking twice, against the physical
card rather than from memory.

## Section 1B — the identification type you circle for a family member

The "circle one of Pink NRIC / Blue NRIC / Birth Certificate Number / FIN"
instruction is in **Section 1B**, which is for family members sharing the same
NRIC address — not for the main applicant.

This matters, because family members do not have to be citizens even though
the main applicant does. The options mean:

- **Pink NRIC** — Singapore Citizen. Pink card.
- **Blue NRIC** — Singapore Permanent Resident. Blue card.
- **Birth Certificate Number** — a child not yet issued an NRIC. In Singapore
  the NRIC is issued at 15, so normally a child under 15.
- **FIN** — Foreign Identification Number, on a work pass, long-term visit
  pass or student pass.

How to decide: look at the physical card in that person's hand and match the
colour. The colour is the answer. Do not infer it from how long they have
lived here — a long-time resident can still hold a blue card.

## Section 1B — who counts as a family member here

Section 1B asks for family members **sharing the same NRIC address as the main
applicant**. For each one: name as in NRIC, their identification number,
relationship to the main applicant (husband, wife, father, mother, son,
daughter and so on), and optionally a mobile number.

Why it is asked: CHAS is assessed on the **household**, not the individual.
Who you list determines whose income is counted and how many people it is
divided between.

The test is the **address on the NRIC**, not the family relationship. A child
who has moved out and changed their NRIC address is not listed here, even
though they are family. Someone whose NRIC still shows this address is.

Two things worth knowing before you fill this in. You are confirming you have
told these people you are applying on their behalf. And if you give a family
member's mobile number, that person may get an SMS when the form is submitted.

If the household is complicated — someone works overseas, someone is
temporarily elsewhere, a tenant rents a spare room — that is a question for
the CHAS hotline, not something to guess at.

## Dwelling Type — the four options and what they mean

Dwelling Type is in Section 1A and is **based on the NRIC address**. The
printed options are:

- **HDB Flat**
- **Private Housing (Including Executive Condo)**
- **Institution (MOH/MSF licensed home)**
- **Others** — with a blank line to write what it is

Note what is *not* here: the form does not ask how many rooms the flat has.
If you are looking for a 3-room or 4-room option, there isn't one. Tick HDB
Flat.

Why it is asked: for a household with no income, the tier is set from the
Annual Value of the home, so the kind of home matters. Tick the one matching
where the NRIC says you live, not a home you used to own or are about to move
into.

## Rental Status of Residence — and the one that catches people out

The printed options in Section 1A are:

- **Renting from Government**
- **Renting from Open Market**
- **Not Rented (e.g. bought or owned)**

The trap is a flat you are still paying for. **A flat bought with a housing
loan you are still repaying is "Not Rented".** You own it; you owe money on
it. Those are different things, and people tick "renting" because money still
goes out every month.

The other two: "Renting from Government" means renting directly from HDB under
a public rental scheme. "Renting from Open Market" means renting from a
private landlord — which can still be an HDB flat.

If you genuinely do not know, the answer is on the lease or tenancy agreement.
Do not guess: this is a field you are about to sign a declaration over.

## Contact details — mobile and home phone

Section 1A asks for a mobile number and a home phone number, plus an optional
email address and a mailing address if it differs from the NRIC address.

The form states the main applicant must provide **either a mobile number or a
home phone number**. One of the two is required — not both.

A Singapore mobile is eight digits starting with 8 or 9. If the applicant does
not use a mobile phone, giving a family member's number is normal and
acceptable — but tell that person, because they will receive messages about
the application.

Worth knowing: the form says an incomplete application missing the applicant's
contact details will be **returned** for completion before it can be
processed. A blank here costs weeks.

## Section 2B — the consent and declaration (STOP HERE)

This is the legally significant part. It is not a formality, and it should
never be signed by someone who has not understood it. On the Dec 2025 form it
is **Section 2B**, headed "Consent and Declaration by Main Applicant".

By signing you are agreeing to two distinct things.

**The consent.** That the Government, IRAS and the CPF Board may collect, use
and disclose your Personal Information — which the form defines broadly:
income information, CPF account balances and withdrawal details, financial
data, household structure, social assistance history, medical reports. This
is what replaces a self-declared income field. You are authorising a
government-to-government lookup of your financial records. The consent stays
valid until you withdraw it in writing, which takes up to 7 working days.

**The declaration.** That all the information you provided is true and
accurate. The form then states plainly what happens if it is not: you and your
family members may be **liable to repay in full** the value of any assistance
granted, including administrative expenses, and **may face criminal
prosecution**.

That is the real reason to stop here. The fields being declared over are
exactly the ones people most often get wrong — dwelling type, rental status,
and who is in the household. An automated helper should not walk anyone
through this step, and nobody should sign it because software said to. Re-read
the fields above, and bring in a family member or call the hotline first.

(If someone is signing on the applicant's behalf, their version is Section 2C,
with the same consequences plus a declaration of what authorises them to act.)

## Section 3 — signature, thumbprint, and what makes a form incomplete

Section 3 holds the main applicant's name, the signature or thumbprint with a
date, and the representative's name where someone signs on the applicant's
behalf. A **thumbprint is explicitly allowed** in place of a signature.

Above it the form restates the responsibilities being accepted: that you have
informed all the family members listed that you are applying on their behalf,
and that you are authorised to do so.

The form will be **returned unprocessed** if it lacks the signature or
thumbprint, or the applicant's contact details.

The same stop applies here as to Section 2B, more strongly for a thumbprint:
an applicant who cannot sign their name is an applicant who should have a
trusted person present.

## The interpreter line

At the end of Section 3: "If the above signatory does not read English, please
state the name and details of the interpreter below", with blanks for the
interpreter's name, NRIC and contact number.

This is worth noticing. The government already accepts that these forms cannot
be completed unaided by everyone who needs them, and has built a human assist
layer into the paper itself. That line is the paper version of what this
product does — with the important difference that a human interpreter can
witness a signature and software cannot.

## Sections 4A and 4B — when the applicant cannot submit for themselves

If the main applicant is 21 or over, lacks the mental capacity to submit the
form, has no donee under a Lasting Power of Attorney and no court-appointed
deputy, and has no Singapore Citizen family member at the same NRIC address,
then Sections 4A and 4B apply. Section 4B requires certification by a
registered medical practitioner.

This is well past what an app should guide anyone through. If this is the
situation, the next step is the CHAS hotline or a social worker, not a form.

## What to do when a field is genuinely unclear

Sometimes the honest answer is "ask". CHAS has a public hotline —
1800-275-2427 (1800-ASK-CHAS) — and participating clinics, Community Centres,
CDCs, polyclinics and public hospitals hold the form and can often help.
HOMES Operations officers may also contact the applicant for supporting
documents.

Guessing on a form you are about to sign a truth declaration over is the one
move that turns a slow afternoon into a repayment demand.
